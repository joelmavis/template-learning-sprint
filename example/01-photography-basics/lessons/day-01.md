# L01 · Exposure triangle

- Status: completed

## Learning objectives

- Explain how aperture, shutter speed, and ISO affect an exposure.
- Choose a safer handheld setting for indoor action.

## Learning content

Exposure is controlled by three linked choices. Aperture changes how much light passes through the lens and influences depth of field. Shutter speed controls how long light reaches the sensor and whether movement is frozen or blurred. ISO changes amplification: raising it can make a dim scene usable but usually adds noise. A practical order is to protect the creative constraint first—motion or depth of field—then raise ISO only as much as needed.

## Practice

For a moving person indoors, choose between A: 1/30 s, f/2.8, ISO 800 and B: 1/125 s, f/2.8, ISO 3200. Explain the trade-off.

## Learner submission

I chose A because ISO 800 should look cleaner, although the person may blur.

## Feedback

The noise trade-off was identified correctly, but the task is a moving person. B is safer because 1/125 s reduces motion blur; some extra noise is preferable to an unusably blurred subject.

## Adaptation

Add a quick shutter-speed check before the next shoot. Mastery remains developing.
